Below are **neuroscience-backed techniques** that reliably improve **semester exam performance**, with the *why* and *how to apply* for each.

---

## 1. Active Recall (Most Important)

**Brain basis:** Strengthens neural retrieval pathways instead of passive recognition.
**How to use:**

* Close notes and write everything you remember.
* Use past-year questions and self-made tests.
* End every study session with a 5–10 min recall dump.

**Rule:** If you’re rereading, you’re undertraining your brain.

---

## 2. Spaced Repetition

**Brain basis:** Works with the forgetting curve; strengthens long-term memory via synaptic consolidation.
**How to use:**

* Revise topics at increasing intervals: Day 1 → Day 3 → Day 7 → Day 21.
* Use flashcards (Anki-style) or revision schedules.

**Rule:** Short, repeated exposure beats long cramming.

---

## 3. Interleaving (Mixed Practice)

**Brain basis:** Forces the brain to discriminate between concepts, improving transfer and problem-solving.
**How to use:**

* Mix similar topics (e.g., OS scheduling + deadlocks).
* Solve mixed-question sets instead of chapter-wise blocks.

**Rule:** Confusion during study = stronger learning later.

---

## 4. Feynman Technique

**Brain basis:** Reveals gaps by forcing simplification and restructuring of knowledge.
**How to use:**

1. Explain the topic as if teaching a 12-year-old.
2. Identify where you struggle.
3. Relearn only that part.
4. Re-explain.

**Rule:** If you can’t teach it simply, you don’t know it.

---

## 5. Elaboration (Why & How Thinking)

**Brain basis:** Builds associative networks, improving recall cues.
**How to use:**

* Ask “Why does this work?” and “How is this different?”
* Connect theory to real-world or code examples.

---

## 6. Dual Coding

**Brain basis:** Combines verbal + visual memory systems for stronger encoding.
**How to use:**

* Convert text into flowcharts, diagrams, timelines.
* Redraw diagrams from memory.

**Rule:** Don’t copy diagrams—recreate them.

---

## 7. Retrieval + Error-Based Learning

**Brain basis:** Errors trigger stronger synaptic correction than correct answers.
**How to use:**

* Attempt questions *before* studying.
* Maintain an “error notebook” and revise only mistakes.

---

## 8. Pomodoro with Cognitive Fatigue Control

**Brain basis:** Prefrontal cortex fatigues after ~45–60 minutes.
**How to use:**

* 45 min deep study + 10 min break.
* No phone during breaks (walk/stretch instead).

---

## 9. Sleep-Dependent Memory Consolidation

**Brain basis:** Hippocampus → neocortex transfer happens during deep sleep.
**How to use:**

* Study hard topics before sleep.
* Minimum 7–8 hours during exam prep.

**Rule:** Sleeping is studying.

---

## 10. Exam Simulation (Context-Dependent Memory)

**Brain basis:** Memory retrieval improves when learning context matches exam context.
**How to use:**

* Timed practice, same chair, same pen.
* Morning practice if exam is in morning.

---

## 11. Stress Regulation (Yerkes–Dodson Law)

**Brain basis:** Moderate stress boosts performance; high stress impairs recall.
**How to use:**

* 2–3 minutes of slow breathing (4–6 breaths/min).
* Light exercise before study.

---

## 12. Weekly Meta-Review

**Brain basis:** Metacognition improves strategy selection and efficiency.
**How to use:**

* Every Sunday, answer:

  * What worked?
  * What didn’t?
  * What will I change next week?

---
